          How every figure here is calculated

          Creative field

          Creative employment divided by creative plus unrelated employment. Graduates enrolled in further education are out of the denominator whatever their employment. From 2023 internships and continuing employment are out too, following the Department of Education guidance SCAD adopted that year, so figures either side of 2023 are not strictly comparable.

          Employed or further education

          Employed plus further education, divided by that plus unemployed. Unemployed counts only an explicit unemployed or a No in the employment field. Graduates with no recorded outcome are never in the denominator. Internships and continuing employment count as positive in 2021 and 2022 and are excluded from 2023.

          Knowledge rate

          The share of graduates for whom the study recorded any outcome. Every other rate is computed on those graduates only. The graduates a study fails to find tend to be those with no visible professional footprint, so the ones who are found skew toward stable, visible employment, and every rate here is likely to be flattering, more so in the later graduation years where coverage is lower.

          Wage estimate is not earnings

          No salary data exists in the alumni files. Each graduate carries the national median wage of the occupation their job title was matched to, from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, May 2025. This describes the wage level of the occupations graduates enter, not what anyone is paid, and an all-career median overstates a first destination.

          Rolled-up programs

          A program that was renamed or absorbed another is reported under one name, so its history runs across all five years instead of breaking in two. Advertising carries Advertising and Branding; Graphic Design carries Graphic Design and Visual Experience; Interactive Design and Game Development carries Game Development; Film and Television carries Cinematography and Editing. The majors are still recorded on every graduate, and Break this down shows them apart.

          Small programs

          Where a denominator is under ten, one record moves the rate by ten points or more. Those cells are marked. Treat them as a count, not a rate.
