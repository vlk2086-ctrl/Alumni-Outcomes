          Alumni outcomes reporting

          About This Data

          Disclaimer and methodological note accompanying the alumni outcomes figures for spring graduates, 2021 to 2025.

          Who is counted

          These figures cover spring graduates only, in the graduation years shown. Graduates who completed in other terms are not included, so the counts here are not a full graduating class and will not match degree-conferral totals.

          Within each spring cohort, all graduates are included. The figures cover full-time and part-time students, international and domestic students, and every graduate regardless of whether they received federal student aid or were eligible to receive it. No graduate is excluded from a denominator on the basis of enrollment status, citizenship or residency, or financial aid status. This is a deliberate difference from federal outcome measures such as the College Scorecard and gainful employment reporting, which are limited to Title IV aid recipients and are therefore not comparable to the rates shown here.

          Where the data comes from

          Outcomes are gathered by SCAD through its own alumni follow-up study, which records each cohort's status twelve months after graduation. It is an internal study, not an audited or third-party survey, and it is not a census. The knowledge rate shown beside every figure is the share of graduates for whom any outcome was recorded, and all other rates are computed on those graduates alone. Graduates a follow-up study fails to reach tend to be the ones with the least visible professional footprint, so every rate reported here is more likely to overstate outcomes than to understate them, and more so in the years where coverage is lower. Read every rate against its knowledge rate.

          No salary data is collected, and no figure here is a salary

          SCAD does not collect earnings from alumni, and no figure in this reporting is a reported or verified wage. The wage estimate assigns each graduate the national median annual wage of the occupation their recorded job title was matched to, as published by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics in the May 2025 Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics.

          It describes what those occupations pay nationally, across all experience levels, not what any SCAD graduate earns and not what a first job after graduation pays, which is typically well below an all-career median. The Bureau of Labor Statistics excludes the self-employed and owners of unincorporated businesses from these estimates, so for graduates running their own studios or practices the figure reflects the wider labor market rather than their own income.

          The figure reported for a program is the average of those occupational medians across its graduates. Over all five years it averages every graduate from 2021 through 2025 together; for a single graduation year it averages only that year's graduates. It is an average of medians, not a median of salaries.

          How an occupation was selected for each graduate

          Occupation assignment begins with a rule set applied uniformly. Each record runs through four steps in order, and the first step that produces a match is the one used:

          1. The recorded job title, matched against an ordered list of title patterns running from most specific to most generic. Generic supervisory words such as manager and supervisor are evaluated last, so a title that names actual work is never overridden by one that does not. This resolves the large majority of records.
          2. For ownership titles such as Owner, Founder, Proprietor, President and CEO, the employer's line of work, read from industry-specific words in the organization name. Ownership titles state who holds a business rather than what the work is, and mapping them to an executive occupation attaches an executive wage to a two-person studio, so they are not treated as an occupation in their own right.
          3. For graduates recorded as employed in a creative field whose title is generic or unmatched, the occupation their program of study trains for.
          4. The employer's line of work again, now including broader terms such as design and arts. These are held until last because they describe an occupation less reliably than the program of study does.

          Where no step resolved, the record carries no wage estimate rather than an assumed one. Those records remain in the population and in the coverage figures, and are excluded only from the wage estimate.

          The rule set is a starting point, not the final word. Every assignment was then reviewed against the recorded job title and employer, and corrected by hand where the rule had read the job wrongly. Occupations set in that review are marked as such on the record. Every record stores the rule or the review that produced its occupation, so any individual assignment can be traced and audited.

          No location data

          These figures carry no geography. Where a graduate lives or works is not reported, and the figures cannot be read by campus, city, state or region. The wage estimates are national medians and are not adjusted for local cost of living or regional pay differences, so they will understate the wage level of an occupation in a high-cost market and overstate it in a low-cost one.

          Comparability across years

          Definitions changed during the period shown. From 2023, following U.S. Department of Education guidance, internships and continuing prior employment are excluded from the outcome measures. In 2021 and 2022 they are counted. Figures on either side of 2023 are not strictly comparable. Programs that were renamed, or that absorbed another major, are reported under a single name so their history runs unbroken, and the majors behind them can be viewed separately.

          Small numbers

          Where a denominator falls under ten, one graduate moves the rate by ten points or more. Those figures are marked and should be read as counts rather than rates. They should not be set beside large programs as though the two carried equal weight.

          Point in time

          These figures are a snapshot of the alumni files taken in October 2026. Records are occasionally corrected after publication, so an individual figure may differ from a number published previously.

          How each rate is defined

          Creative field. Creative employment divided by creative plus unrelated employment. Graduates enrolled in further education are out of the denominator whatever their employment. From 2023 internships and continuing employment are out too.

          Employed or further education. Employed plus further education, divided by that plus unemployed. Unemployed counts only an explicit unemployed or a No in the employment field. Graduates with no recorded outcome are never in the denominator.

          Knowledge rate. The share of graduates for whom the study recorded any outcome. Every other rate is computed on those graduates only.

          Questions about the method or any individual figure go to the Office of Assessment and Curriculum.
